Focus Recommendations

M — Materials & Reagents

How to count GHS pictograms

  • For each reagent (solid or liquid) used anywhere (Up-front syntheses, sample preparation, and analysis) count the distinct hazard pictograms shown on the manufacturer’s safety data sheets (SDS) or on the bottle label.
  • If you use multiple reagents, enable “Multiple reagents are used” and enter the pictogram count and per-sample amount for each reagent.
  • Enter amounts used per sample
  • If a reagent SDS pictogram includes the signal word "Danger", it is counted as one additional pictogram. SDS with the signal word "Warning" or no signal word are not assigned an additional pictogram.

Examples

  • A solvent labeled with Flame, Exclamation, and no signal word→ enter 2.
  • A solvent labeled with Flame, Exclamation, and Warning signal word→ enter 2.
  • A solvent labeled with Flame, Exclamation, and Danger signal word→ enter 3.
  • Water → 0 pictograms.

Note

The M pillar uses a hazard-weighted effective volume so simply diluting a hazardous material with water does not artificially improve the M score. The R pillar uses the actual raw total waste volume, so there’s no double penalization.

How to choose

Use CHEM21 selection guide or another comparable framework, provided that such guidelines are explicitly stated and cited. Water is the greenest solvent

Examples:

  • All: Mix of renewable sources/green/recommended (e.g., Water + EtOH).
  • Some: Mix of green + fossil/problematic organics (e.g., Water+ ACN, EtOH + ACN, green IL + ACN).
  • None: All organics fossil/problematic (e.g., ACN, hexane).

E — Energy & Emissions

Used only to display CO₂e inside the center; does not affect the E score. CO₂e = Total energy × Grid factor.
Energy per sample Σ(P×t/n) = 0.0000 kWh/sample
Instrument (select or type)

Grid factor value is based on institution’s country. List all instruments used per sample including synthesis, preparation, and analysis

How energy/sample is computed

Energy per sample = Σ (Power in kW (P)×Operation time in h (t)÷Samples processed (n)) across rows.

  • Power (kW): Select instruments from the list provided, MERITS fills in an average power from different manufacturers. These values can be edited if the user knows their instrument’s exact consumption or choose the Custom option for instruments not in the list.
  • Operation Time (h): The time the instrument is actively running.
  • Number of samples: The count of samples processed in that same time window by that instrument. The only exception is the up-front synthesis, where the number of samples entered should reflect how many analyses can be performed with the total amount of material synthesized.

Examples

  • Microwave digester: If it draws ~2.72 kW and digests a batch of 25 samples in 1.0 h, enter P=2.72, t=1.0, n=25 → contributes (2.72×1.0/25)=0.1088 kWh/sample.
  • Chromatography: A 30-min run for one vial → t=0.5, n=1. If that vial has multiple analytes, samples stay 1 because analyte count is handled in the T-pillar. Also for power, two rows should be added, one for the chromatograph and the other for the detector, if they are listed independently in the drop-down options.
  • Number of samples exception: CNTs hydrothermally synthesized in an oven for 24 h → P=0.68 t=24, n=200. n = 200 represents the number of samples that can be analyzed with the total synthesized batch, based on the methodology per-analysis consumption.

R — Residuals (Waste)

What to include

Select the total waste generated per sample during:

  • Sample preparation
  • Analytical instrumental run (e.g., mobile phase or cuvette)
  • Upstream syntheses

Upstream syntheses

Allocate upstream syntheses waste using the same (n) concept established for upstream energy allocation in the E pillar, as follows:

Synthesis waste generated per sample = total waste generated during batch synthesis ÷ number of samples that can be processed by the batch

Example: A batch yields 50.0 mL of CQDs, generates 200.0 mL waste, and each analysis consumes 0.5 mL of CQDs. The batch supports 50.0/0.5 = 100.0 analyses, so the allocated synthesis waste is 200.0/100.0 = 2.0 mL/sample. Add this 2.0 mL to the waste generated during the sample preparation and instrumental run.

Do not include

The original sample amount; it is handled in the T pillar.

Disposables vs reusable labware

Reusable materials are durable items that are cleaned and used again: glassware (beakers, flasks, funnels, cylinders), reusable sorbents, glass chromatography columns, etc.

Disposable materials are single-use items: pipette tips, microcentrifuge (Eppendorf) tubes, syringes, syringe tips/needles/filters/membranes. For kits like QuEChERS count each disposable used, don't say it is a single kit so number is one

How to choose an option

  • ≤1 small disposable / Reusable materials: Entirely reusable workflow or with one minor disposable per sample (e.g., a single tip and chip).
  • Some disposables (2–4): A few single-use items per sample.
  • Many disposables (>4): Several disposable items per sample.

I — Integration

How to choose the in-situ level

Select how close the measurement is to the process or environment:

  • Off-line (ex-situ): Sample is taken from the process or environment and sent to be analyzed in a separate lab/location. Examples: purchasing finished tablets for testing; withdrawing plasma or production samples, then storing and/or transporting them to the QC lab.
  • At-line: Sample is manually withdrawn from the process but analyzed near the line (same room/next to the reactor/on the production floor). Examples: bench-top FTIR/NMR, smartphone-assisted TLC.
  • On-line: Sample is automatically diverted from the process into an analyzer (often via a bypass loop/side-stream), measured, then returned to the process or discarded. Examples: a side-stream flow cell with HPLC or a spectrometer that continuously samples from the pipeline.
  • In-line: Sensor is directly in the process stream, and measurement is performed without sample removal. Examples: immersed electrochemical electrodes, pH/Raman/NIR probes.

Note: There is no separate storage/transport criterion because these are reflected by the in-situ level, automation, and their impacts can appear in sample size (T pillar) and energy (E). Hence, adding a separate criterion would double-penalize effects that often depend on external clinical/production logistics rather than the analytical method design itself.

How to count “steps”

Count only distinct sample-prep unit operations that actively transform/condition or separate the sample. Do not count “linking” actions.

  • Count it: sonication, vortex mixing, centrifugation, filtration, evaporation (rotavap/N₂/oven), precipitation, digestion, solid–liquid extraction, SPE (treat one continuous cartridge run as a single step), lyophilization.
  • Don’t count: transfers between vessels, routine bring-to-volume, serial dilutions, simple pipetting/aliquoting, reconstitution, labeling, weighing, passive waiting/standing.

Tip: If a sequence happens on the same device without interruption or intervention (e.g., automated load→wash→elute on SPE cartridge), consider it one step.

T — Throughput

What “Sample size” means

Enter the amount of original sample/matrix you take for analysis after sampling and before any preparation (in g or mL).

  • It is NOT the injection volume into the instrument.
  • It is NOT the total dilution/preparation volume.
  • It is NOT the concentration (e.g., 200 µg/mL).

Examples

  • Tablet (HPLC): 10 mg powdered tablet → dissolve to 50 mL → filter → inject 20 µL.
    Sample size = 0.010 g (10 mg). Not the final volume (50 mL), not 200 µg/mL, and not 20 µL. Even if that 200 µg/mL is a stock for further dilution, the sample size remains 0.010 g.
  • Serum (UV): 0.5 mL serum → protein-precipitate → filter and dilute → fill cuvette.
    Sample size = 0.5 mL.
More than 0.1 mL or g, enter the value.

How to calculate “Samples/hour”

Include the sample preparation (e.g., extraction, precipitation, derivatization) plus the instrument analysis time.

Example

Preparing a tablet dosage form takes 10 minutes and the HPLC run is 5 minutes. The total time per sample = 15 minutes, so Samples/hour = 60/15 = 4.

One-time synthesis (not counted here)

Single, up-front syntheses that serve many samples (e.g., synthesizing CQDs or modified CNTs that took 24 h to make and used for hundreds of analyses) are not part of the per-sample throughput time. Their penalties are reflected in the M, E, R, and S pillars.

S — Safety & Exposure

How to choose this level

  • Minimal (baseline lab PPE): lab coat, eye glasses, single pair of disposable nitrile gloves, closed-toe shoes, ear-loop masks.
  • Moderate: baseline plus at least one of: eye goggles; face shield; flame-resistant or chemical-resistant lab coat; heavy chemical-resistant, cryogenic, heat-resistant, or cut-resistant gloves; double-nitrile gloving for some steps
  • Extensive: air-purifying or air-supplied respirator; full gown/Tyvek suit; dedicated shoes; double gloves as standard in all steps
Any critical hazard not under control will trigger red-flag rule.

Data Confidence & Integrity

How to evaluate completeness

Completeness = 1.00 when all method-specific information required for MERITS is available.

When information is missing, classify the primary omission first:

  • Single-pillar omission (−0.10): one quantitative or categorical input required for one MERITS pillar is unavailable.
  • Cross-pillar omission (−0.20): one quantitative or categorical input required for two or more MERITS pillars is unavailable.

Then subtract −0.10 for each additional independent missing item, regardless of whether that item affects one or several pillars.

Examples

  • Missing centrifugation duration: affects Energy and Throughput → cross-pillar omission = −0.20.
  • Missing acetonitrile quantity: affects Materials and Residuals → cross-pillar omission = −0.20.
  • Missing sample amount: affects only Throughput → single-pillar omission = −0.10.
  • Missing renewable/recommended classification: affects only Materials → single-pillar omission = −0.10.
  • Missing centrifugation duration and sample amount: primary cross-pillar omission −0.20 plus one additional independent omission −0.10 → total deduction −0.30 (Completeness = 0.70).
  • Missing acetonitrile quantity, centrifugation duration, and sample amount: primary cross-pillar omission −0.20 plus two additional independent omissions −0.20 → total deduction −0.40 (Completeness = 0.60).
  • Missing sample amount and material classification: primary single-pillar omission −0.10 plus one additional independent omission −0.10 → total deduction −0.20 (Completeness = 0.80).

How to evaluate evidence quality

Evidence quality = 1.00 when all entered data are reported, traceable, taken from SDS or specification information, well documented, or supported by directly relevant published evidence.

Apply −0.10 for each independent entry that is based on an assumption, a non-method-specific proxy, or an unsupported categorical claim.

Examples

  • Centrifugation capacity assumed as 16 samples because the centrifuge model is not specified → one assumption = −0.10.
  • An ionic liquid is classified as green without directly relevant published evidence → one unsupported categorical claim = −0.10.
  • Centrifugation capacity is assumed, and sample amount is estimated from the linear range and reported preparation steps → two independent assumptions = −0.20.
Completeness: What method-specific data is missing? Evidence quality: How reliable are entered data? Final score multiplied by (0.85 + 0.15·Average).

Method & Notes

MERITS (Materials, Energy, Residuals, Integration, Throughput, Safety) is a composite index for scoring the greenness of analytical methods.

  • Color legend: ≥75 = Excellent (Green), 50–74 = Acceptable (Yellow), <50 = Needs improvement (Glowing red).
  • Each pillar is a colored arc around the centre and the score is shown from 0-100.
  • Each arc has sub-bands whose colors reflect the contribution of each sub-criterion to that pillar.
  • The overall MERITS score is the geometric mean of the six pillar scores which reduces compensation by excellent pillars.
  • Small lower bounds are applied to the Materials pillar (5%) and Energy pillar (8%) to prevent zero-value collapse while preserving strong penalties for poor performance.
  • Safety pillar has a red-flag rule if any critical hazard is not controlled.
  • Focus recommendations highlight the two lowest-scoring pillars that fall in the yellow or glowing red range and suggest improvements based on the sub-criteria with the greatest negative impact.
  • A confidence factor (from data completeness and evidence quality) gently scales the final score (0.85+0.15⋅D). The value is shown under the total score as “Confidence (%)”.
  • The CO₂e per sample is estimated from total instrument energy × grid factor and displayed for informational purposes; it does not affect the score.
